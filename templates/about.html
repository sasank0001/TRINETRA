Project Overview & Motivation

Threat Intelligence

Phishing remains the preeminent vector for unauthorized account compromise, credential harvesting, and enterprise infrastructure breaches. Attackers systematically exploit deceptive domain naming conventions, homoglyph characters, subdomain nesting, and obfuscated paths to masquerade as credible banking and enterprise institutions.

TRINETHRA is engineered to provide pre-execution cyber defense. By applying pure lexical feature extraction coupled with an ensemble Random Forest machine learning classifier, TRINETHRA evaluates the intrinsic structure of a URL to deliver rapid, explainable risk verdicts without ever establishing an outbound HTTP connection to the destination host.

Primary Engineering Objectives:

Deterministic Lexical Extraction: Evaluates 41 structural URL features without DNS resolution or network roundtrips.
High-Accuracy Machine Learning: Detects zero-day deceptive links using an ensemble of 120 Random Forest decision trees (94%+ accuracy).
Explainable AI (XAI) Scoring: Provides transparent, human-readable indicator explanations rather than an opaque black-box verdict.
Passive Zero-Execution Sandbox: Guarantees zero risk of client-side drive-by malware execution or outbound server beaconing.

Brand Identity: The Philosophy of TRINETHRA

Cultural Heritage & Modern Science

Trinethra (त्रिनेत्र) derives from the ancient Sanskrit concept of the Three-Eyed Vision — representing supreme perception, intuitive vigilance, and the ability to look beyond surface illusions to see ultimate truth.

In cybersecurity, deceptive phishing links disguise themselves beneath authentic-looking brand names and valid SSL certificates. The TRINETHRA emblem incorporates the vertical third eye intersecting the classical Tripundra (three horizontal Namam lines), symbolizing constant vigilance, analytical foresight, and modern cryptographic protection.

Machine Learning Methodology & Dataset

Research Standard

The predictive pipeline uses a Random Forest Classifier composed of 120 decision trees. The training corpus aggregates verified phishing records from the PhiUSIIL Phishing URL Dataset (UCI Machine Learning Repository #967), curated research phishing corpora, and benign web domains from the Tranco Top 1M authority.
